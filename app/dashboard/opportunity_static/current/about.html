HOW TO READ PREDICT

About this board

Compare the price offered by a market with a sportsbook-based estimate of fair value, after modeled fees.

What you’re looking at

Predict is a read-only research tool for pregame winners, spreads and totals. Opportunities shows complete main-line bets with positive estimated Net EV, ranked by Opportunity Score. Browse odds orders games by start time and groups the available main winner, spread and total lines together, including zero and negative EV. Open a game to see its alternate lines and exact-price comparisons.

Select an opportunity row to see the available market and sportsbook prices for that exact pick and line. Hover, focus or tap an underlined value to see its calculation. The game name opens the wider game view.

The idea behind the edge

Our working hypothesis is that major sportsbook lines contain useful information about a game’s chances. If those lines are a good probability benchmark, a better price in a peer-to-peer market can have positive expected value after fees. That is a modeling assumption—not a claim that Vegas is always right, that these books are the world’s most accurate, or that every apparent edge is real.

We convert both sides of each eligible sportsbook’s exact line into implied probabilities, then normalize them to remove the margin. For each pick, Predict uses the lowest resulting win probability from eligible FanDuel, DraftKings, BetMGM and Caesars pairs. This is conservative relative to those book estimates; all of them can still be wrong. Research documents biases and inefficiencies in sportsbook prices, so we treat the reference as an estimate rather than ground truth. Levitt’s NFL market research ↗

For a simple win-or-lose position: Net EV = p × net winning receipt ÷ committed cash + (1 − p) × net losing receipt ÷ committed cash − 1. Fees are included in the receipts and cash committed. No forecast here eliminates variance or protects a single bet from losing.

Example: if the fair chance is 50% and $100 committed returns $210 after fees on a win and $0 on a loss, the estimated EV is 0.50 × $210 ÷ $100 − 1 = +5%.

What the numbers mean

Price
The stored offer price. Contract markets also show a payout multiple or cents/percentage price, with American odds in parentheses. A percentage price is a quote equivalent, not the model’s estimate of your chance of winning. Aggregate-feed conversions are identified in the price explanation.
After fees
The modeled return on committed cash after applicable documented fees. The calculation uses an internal comparison basis of up to $100, not a recommended stake. Private account charges, rebates, available size and slippage can differ.
Fair odds
A probability-based reference obtained by removing one sportsbook’s margin across the matching outcomes. We choose the lowest fair win probability among eligible complete FanDuel, DraftKings, BetMGM and Caesars pairs for that exact bet. Book order breaks ties. This gives the lowest EV estimate among those inputs; it does not establish the true probability or guarantee a conservative bound. Underneath, Book (odds) identifies that sportsbook and its actual posted price. Fair odds are derived; they are not an offer you can place.
Net EV
The probability-weighted return after modeled fees. For example, +5% means an estimated average $5 return above cost for every $100 committed over repeated equivalent outcomes under the model. Any individual bet can lose, and an incorrect probability estimate can make the apparent edge disappear.
Opportunity Score
A ranking based on modeled return and payoff risk. It is not a win probability, guaranteed profit or suggested stake. The calculation is available on the score itself.

Arbitrage: both sides, after fees

An arbitrage acquires complementary outcomes for less than their combined payoff. It does not require a sportsbook probability estimate. Predict checks market-to-market pairs with fees on both legs and market-to-sportsbook pairs with fees on the market leg. Sportsbook placement commission is assumed to be zero; account charges and taxes are outside this comparison.

The Arbs page shows both picks, their posted and after-fee prices, and the lower modeled profit across the two decisive completed-game outcomes. Its illustrative allocation uses up to $100 combined and recalculates fees and legal quantity increments on each leg. For example, $48 and $49 buying opposite $100 payouts commit $97 and return $100 in either decisive outcome: $3 ÷ $97 = 3.09%, before any additional charges.

These are conditional price-arb candidates, not verified risk-free trades. The selections must cover opposite sides of the same event, period and exact line under matching scoring rules. Pushes, ties, voids, cancellations and different venue settlements can break the hedge. Those exceptional outcomes are not covered by the displayed two-state minimum. A nearby line can leave an uncovered outcome; we do not combine different lines.

Instant-fill maximum: unknown. The current stored quotes do not establish immediately executable size on both sides. The $100 allocation is a calculation basis, not a maximum fill or suggested stake. Both official listings and current order books must be checked before acting.

Price control for EV; immediate liquidity for arbs

For an EV position, use a limit price that preserves the edge: fill at that price or better, or remain unfilled. A limit controls price; it does not ensure liquidity, a complete fill, or the fees associated with a resting maker order. This board models the captured taker-fee basis, not a promised maker discount. Kalshi’s limit-order explanation ↗

For an arb, the goal is to take liquidity immediately on both sides, not leave a resting order waiting for the second leg. Verify how much fills now and the worst price that preserves a positive combined return. A market order can consume several price levels and slip past the displayed quote. A marketable limit with immediate execution conditions can cap price where supported, but can also fail or fill partially. Neither approach makes two venues atomic: one leg can fill while the other disappears.

Order names and unfilled-remainder behavior differ by venue. Check the official order ticket and rules: some immediate orders cancel the remainder, while others can leave it resting. No order type guarantees a cross-venue arb. Polymarket US order documentation ↗

What the board does not prove

Prices may be delayed, withdrawn or changed. A stored quote does not establish that you can trade at that price or in any particular size. Price comparisons require the same event, selection and exact line; a nearby handicap is a different bet.

The current calculation models a decisive completed game. Pushes, voids, cancellations and other exceptional settlements are excluded from its win/loss probabilities. Verify the venue’s actual rules, fees and account terms.

Refreshing the page reads stored data; it does not request a new quote from a venue. When viewing a saved database snapshot, the data stays frozen even though games leave the pregame board at kickoff. Source and receipt times are available in comparison details.

Before placing a bet

Verify the exact event, pick, line, current price, available size, fees and settlement rules in the venue’s app. Predict does not place trades or manage your account. This information is for research, not personalized financial advice or a promise of returns. Betting involves risk, including losing your entire stake; use only money you can afford to lose.
